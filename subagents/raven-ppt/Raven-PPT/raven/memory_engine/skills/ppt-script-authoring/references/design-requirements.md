# What a finished page owes

You can see the page. Crowding, small type, a stretched figure, a ragged edge — look and
say so. This is not that list.

This is what is easy to get **backwards**: room that is not a defect, a defect that does
not look like one, and a fix that would make the page worse.

## Empty room is two things

`oversized_shape` — a shape bigger than what it holds. A panel around one sentence, a card
at twice the height of its three lines. **Measuring fixes it.**

`underfilled_page` — every shape fits and the page is still a third empty. **Shrinking
fixes nothing**; it wants more said, or one page instead of two.

Which one: *would making the shape smaller fill the page?* Yes → the first. No → the second.

## Not defects

- A row of cards is drawn to the tallest. The short ones carry slack **by design**. Report
  the row being taller than its tallest card, not the short card having space.
- On a **cloned** page (the line under this says which) the badges and marks are the
  template's — the author cannot add an icon there. Still report a mark whose meaning is
  wrong, or a style that changes partway down one list.
- A table narrower than its band is a missing `weights`, one word. Say it does not reach
  the row above; do not redesign the page.
- **One tinted row in a table needs no legend.** It is the author saying the argument
  rests on that row, and every reader takes it that way. Alternating tints are a rail and
  need none either.

## Defects that do not announce themselves

- `listed` — four or five `label: sentence` claims stacked in one box. They read as a list
  to be read out, not as four things. One claim, one block.
- `listed` — a table whose cells are words. A grid is for comparing **down a column**; rows
  of phrases each naming a thing are cards in a grid's clothing. A tick or a filled scale
  counts as a figure; the words "supported / not supported" do not.
- `table` — a tinted ground cut to the band while the table is cut to its content, so
  colour sticks out past the grid.
- `alignment` — a short column hung from the top beside a full-height figure. It is
  centred against its neighbour, not missing content.

## Two rules

- Air goes **between** components, not inside them. The gap between must be plainly wider
  than any gap within, or the eye finds no boundary.
- A figure is evidence: its text legible at the size given, its aspect kept, not cropped
  through the point, and the template's stock photos replaced or dropped.

## The claim

You are given the page's planned claim. A page that became a list of facts with the point
missing is a defect, and so is a page whose loudest element is not what the claim is about.
