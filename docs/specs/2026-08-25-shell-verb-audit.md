# The twenty-one shell verbs, one verdict each

Status: an audit, not a plan for any one piece. Every number is measured on
`3b68ff72` (the `!260` squash, which took the count from 24 to 21).
Scope: `ui/` only. No wire-protocol change, no server change.
Follows: `2026-08-21-page-remaining-legacy.md`, whose Axis 2 asked for exactly
this and stopped at "the number should be visible".

## Why a verdict and not a migration

That document's Endgame conditions the finish on Axis 1 being empty and Axis 2
being **deliberate** -- not zero. Axis 1 and Axis 3 reached zero in the work
since. So what is left for Axis 2 is not "move all 21", it is "be able to say,
for each one, whether it moves or stays and why". A verb kept on purpose costs
nothing; a verb kept because nobody looked is the thing the ratchet exists to
prevent.

## Method, and the two things it corrected

For each verb: read its legacy half in `demo/155-bridge.js`, find the target
that half calls, and -- the step that changed two verdicts -- **grep for every
assignment to that target, not just its definition**. A verb whose target is
reassigned elsewhere is not the one-liner its definition suggests.

That check corrected two rows:

- `closeDetail` reads as `$('#detail').dataset.open = 'false'` at
  `demo/120-capabilities.js:80`, which looks like the cheapest row on the page.
  It is reassigned twice more -- `demo/152-skills.js:88` and
  `demo/153-plugins.js:120` -- each wrapping the previous one
  (`prevCloseDetail`). It is a decorator chain that grows a link per page
  sharing the drawer, and moving it means modelling "who wants to be told the
  shared drawer closed". Not cheap.
- `verb('wsView')` and friends were first counted as uncalled, because the
  survey grepped `shell().x`. The islands also reach the shell as `sh.x`,
  `bridge().x`, and `verb('x')`. All 21 are asked; none is dead weight.

The second is the same fault this migration has now made six times: a proxy for
the thing being counted, reported as the thing being counted.

Seven times. Review caught this document's own `openConn` row counting one
caller when there are three: the two in `features/settings` reach the verb as
`sh.openConn?.()`, which is the very shape the paragraph above says the earlier
survey missed. The row written to demonstrate the corrected method was produced
by the uncorrected one. Every `asked by` cell below is now generated by matching
all four shapes rather than typed out.

## The verdicts

### Retire now -- pure round trips (3)

Each legacy half is one line that calls straight back into another island, and
none of the three targets is ever reassigned:

| verb | legacy half | asked by |
|---|---|---|
| `openCron` | `RavenIslands.cron.open()` | `features/rail`, `shell/navfly` |
| `openXa` | `RavenIslands.xa.open()` | `shell/navfly` |
| `openConn` | `RavenIslands.connections.open()` | `shell/navfly`; `features/settings` twice, as `sh.openConn?.()` |

The legacy layer adds nothing on the way through. The caller imports the target
island instead. No chrome, no shared state, no DOM. This is the only group whose
move is mechanical.

### Keep -- the one round trip that is load-bearing (1)

`markNew` looks identical to the three above: its legacy half is
`RavenIslands.rail.markNew()` and it is never reassigned either. It stays, and
the reason is a direction rather than a behaviour. `features/rail/store.ts`
already imports `mark` from `shell/navfly`, so having navfly import rail's
`markNew` closes the loop -- a dependency the shell currently keeps one-way
becomes a literal import cycle. The seam is what makes the direction hold.

That is the third row this audit's method corrected, and the only one where the
legacy half being trivial was true and still the wrong answer.

### Keep -- the catalogue and the page-level modal (3)

| verb | why it stays |
|---|---|
| `T` | the i18n catalogue lives in `demo/010-kernel.js:35`; every island needs it and none should own it. The seam is the point, and the remaining-legacy document already named it the example of a permanent one. |
| `attNotes` | reads `I18N.ui['gui.att.note']`. Same catalogue, same argument. Retiring it separately from `T` would be arbitrary. |
| `confirmAsk` | a modal over whatever page is up, asked by four different pages (`connections`, `cron`, `settings`, `xa`). It belongs to page chrome by definition -- an island that owned it would be answering for pages it does not own. |

These three are the answer to "deliberate": they stay, and now there is a
sentence saying why.

### Move, with real work -- ranked by ratio

| group | verbs | what makes it work |
|---|---|---|
| settings open-state | `openSet` `closeSet` `setIsOpen` | the settings island asks the legacy layer whether **it** is open. Self-contained chrome: `#setVeil`, `#setModal`, plus a `markNewCurrent()` call. A clean inversion, and the smallest real move on the page. |
| the workspace panel | `showWorkspace` `workspaceSetOpen` `wsShows` `wsView` `wsPick` | five verbs, one domain, biggest single reduction. But `wsTab/wsOpen/wsWide/wsPicked` live at `demo/100-workspace.js:15` with six legacy readers across both layers, and `setWs`/`wsPick` are not state writes -- they touch `#split` and `#wsBtn`, run the desk redirect, and call `drawWs()`/`bumpWs()`. Moving it means the legacy chrome becomes a subscriber. Highest reduction, highest risk. |
| the shared detail drawer | `closeDetail` | needs the decorator chain above replaced by a subscription. One verb, but a design question rather than a move. |
| `useInTask` | `useInTask` | `demo/152-skills.js:12`; puts a prompt into the composer. Plausibly the composer island's, pending a read of what else it touches. |

### Blocked on page chrome (4)

`showPage`, `navState`, `plugRedraw`, `openWebsearch` all reach the capabilities
page's dispatch -- `NAV_OF` at `demo/120-capabilities.js:7`, `extTab`, and
`openCaps`. `navfly.ts` already states that nav marks are decided jointly with
the rail's own buttons, which sit outside any root a module could own. These
four are not independently retirable: they move when page chrome moves, or not
at all.

## What this adds up to

| verdict | count |
|---|---|
| retire now, mechanical | 3 |
| keep, argued | 4 |
| move, real work | 10 |
| blocked on page chrome | 4 |

A floor of **21 to 18** for one low-risk change -- taken in the commit beside
this document -- and a defensible resting point at **18 to 14** if the settings
and drawer groups follow, which are 3 verbs and 1. Driving it to zero is not a goal this audit supports:
eight of the 21 are either permanent by design or gated on a chrome migration
nobody has scoped.
